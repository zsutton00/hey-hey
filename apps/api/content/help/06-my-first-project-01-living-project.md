Onboarding gives you an initial map of your product. It identifies the capabilities you appear to deliver and organizes them into a feature tree.

Treat this as a living document. Some features may turn out to be too broad, too narrow, misplaced, or simply unnecessary. You may discover an important product area that was not obvious during onboarding. That is normal.

The goal is not to design the perfect hierarchy on your first day. The goal is to create enough shared structure that you and the agents can discuss the same product, make decisions in the right context, and improve the model as you learn.

A healthy project changes for good reasons:

- Customer research changes your understanding of a problem.
- A design exposes capabilities that deserve their own features.
- Implementation reveals that apparently related work belongs in separate areas.
- A completed feature is extended, replaced, or connected to another part of the product.

You are building a durable map of what your product delivers and why. Like the product itself, that map becomes more accurate over time.

**Try it:** Open your feature tree and find one feature whose purpose you could explain clearly to a user of your product.

**You're done when:** You have a useful starting map and feel comfortable changing it later.
